# Problem Statement

## Context

Simpli's growth depends on matching two sides: corporate supply of excess inventory, and charity demand for it. The supply side is healthy and growing. Six active company pilots represent more than $4.57M in known value and over 297 pallets, with Cardinal Health alone at 221 pallets. The constraint is the charity side.

## The three layered gap

A production audit of all 198 charities on the platform (April 20, 2026) surfaced three compounding failures.

### Layer 1: Onboarding drop off

Of 198 charities, 89 (45 percent) are Onboarded, 97 (49 percent) are still Invited, and 12 (6 percent) Claimed a profile but never finished. That means 109 charities, 55 percent of the base, received an invitation and never completed onboarding.

The likely cause is the onboarding experience itself: a 13 section profile form with no value signaling, sent through a generic invitation email. Charities are asked for a large amount of data before they see any reason to care.

### Layer 2: Activation drop off

Even among charities that finish onboarding, only about half ever receive a donation. Across the full base, only 45 of 198 charities (22.7 percent) have ever received a donation. Onboarding completion does not equal activation.

Profile completeness is poor even among active charities. Midwest Food Bank, an Onboarded charity that has received donations, had a completely empty Pallet Capacity field. If a large, well resourced food bank leaves a critical matching field blank, smaller charities almost certainly do worse.

### Layer 3: Supply and demand mismatch

Simpli is targeting $150M or more in donations this year, which translates to roughly 1,500 to 3,000 pallets and about 150 pallets per month of charity absorption capacity. That requires 200 or more charities, including large ones that can each absorb dozens of pallets a month. The platform currently has 89 onboarded charities and only 45 that have ever received a donation. The onboarded base needs to roughly double, and per charity throughput needs to rise at the same time.

## Why this is an experience problem, not a tooling problem

Secondary research across industry reports and nonprofit communities pointed to the same root cause. Charities are overloaded with disconnected tools and have no incentive loop tied to directory profiles. They miss opportunities, including donation matches, because nobody surfaces the right information at the right time. Only 1.31 percent of eligible matched giving is actually captured, and 78 percent of donors do not know matching exists. The pattern is awareness and timing, not capability.

Applied to Simpli: a charity will not complete a 13 section form for an abstract promise. It will engage if it can see, early and concretely, what completing its profile unlocks.

## What this means for the solution

The activation layer addresses each layer directly. Value first sequencing attacks the onboarding drop off. A match readiness score and surfaced impact data attack the activation drop off. Faster, lower friction activation across the base attacks the supply and demand mismatch by making the charity network both larger and quicker to mobilize.
